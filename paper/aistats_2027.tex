\begin{abstract}
  We introduce FusedSpaceFed, a federated learning framework designed to mitigate client drift under heterogeneous data distributions through dual-space data fusion. In settings such as medical imaging, client heterogeneity may reflect both different label distributions and site-specific acquisition characteristics. FusedSpaceFed maintains a private convolutional encoder for each client, while the decoder and classifier are shared through federated aggregation. At each client, the original input is combined with the reconstruction produced by the local encoder and the shared decoder, and the resulting fused representation is used for classification. Training alternates between an encoder-only reconstruction warm-up and an end-to-end classification phase. We provide an exact fixed-round decomposition of classifier-parameter gradient dispersion, which separates the dispersion observed without fusion from the variance and covariance terms introduced by the fused representation. The decomposition identifies the variance--covariance condition under which fusion lowers cross-client gradient dispersion at a fixed model state. Experiments show that FusedSpaceFed improves predictive performance under severe non-IID partitions and produces lower empirical classifier-gradient dispersion than the considered federated baselines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Federated Learning (FL) is a decentralized machine learning paradigm where multiple clients collaboratively train a shared model without sharing their private data~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}. This framework is particularly suited to domains such as medical imaging, finance and mobile devices, where privacy regulations or communication constraints prohibit centralized data aggregation~\cite{kairouz2021advances}. In practice, however, the performance of FL systems is severely hindered by the heterogeneity of data distributions across clients, which induces divergence in local model updates and slows convergence.

This is a fundamental challenge in FL: when clients possess non-identically distributed data, their local parameter gradients $\nabla_{\theta} F_i(\theta)$ may diverge from their mean $\nabla_{\theta} F(\theta)=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\nabla_{\theta}F_i(\theta)$, where $\theta$ denotes the shared model parameters and $F_i$ is the objective of client~$i$. We refer to the second moment of these deviations as gradient dissimilarity. Similar drift phenomena have been theoretically analyzed in local and distributed SGD, where statistical heterogeneity introduces bias into local updates and may slow optimization~\cite{stich2019localsgd,woodworth2020minibatch}. This dissimilarity forces federated averaging algorithms to use conservative step sizes and leads to convergence rates that degrade with the degree of heterogeneity.

Traditional approaches mitigate this problem through proximal regularization, control variates, gradient tracking, or personalized model components~\cite{li2018federated,karimireddy2020scaffold,haddadpour2021compression,arivazhagan2019fedper,collins2021fedrep,li2021ditto,li2021fedbn}.
%Traditional approaches mitigate this problem through proximal regularization, control variates, gradient tracking or personalized model components~\cite{li2018federated,karimireddy2020scaffold}.
FusedSpaceFed acts instead on the representation supplied to the classifier: each client combines its original input with a reconstruction generated through a private encoder and a federated decoder. The objective is to reduce, rather than eliminate, the dispersion of client updates induced by heterogeneous local data.

As a guiding example, we will consider the context of medical imaging federation. Here, heterogeneity has a concrete physical origin beyond label imbalance~\cite{sheller2018multi,guan2024federated}. Different clinical sites rely on different scanners, acquisition protocols, reconstruction pipelines, and calibration procedures~\cite{glocker2019machine,dinsdale2021unlearning}. As a result, two images of the same pathology acquired at different hospitals may differ substantially in intensity statistics, contrast and noise characteristics. These instrumental variations introduce systematic site-specific biases that are unrelated to the underlying medical content, yet strongly affect model training.

Consequently, gradients computed at different sites may diverge not because the task differs, but because each client observes the data through a different ``instrument''. From an optimization perspective, this effect amplifies client drift and exacerbates the gradient dissimilarity described above, degrading convergence and generalization.

FusedSpaceFed is motivated by a simple principle: heterogeneity can be addressed not only by modifying the optimizer, but also by modifying the representation supplied to the classifier. Each client retains a private multiscale encoder, whereas the decoder and classifier are synchronized by the server. For an input image $x$, the classifier processes the fused signal $x+D(E_i(x))$. The shared decoder is influenced by all participating clients, while the private encoder preserves client-specific adaptation.

The contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item an asymmetric federated architecture composed of private multiscale encoders, a shared decoder and a shared classifier;
    \item a two-phase local optimization protocol that alternates an encoder-only reconstruction warm-up with classification-only end-to-end training of the encoder, decoder, and classifier;
    \item an exact fixed-round decomposition of classifier-parameter gradient dispersion into the original dispersion, a fusion-induced variance term and a cross-covariance term;
    \item an empirical evaluation on twelve MedMNIST datasets, pathological class partitions, controlled Dirichlet stress tests, and the naturally heterogeneous FEMNIST benchmark.
\end{itemize}

The architectural contribution lies in the coordinated combination of a private multiscale encoder, a federated decoder, additive input-space fusion and per-round two-phase optimization. Together, these components construct a client-conditioned representation while transferring information through the shared reconstruction and classification pathways.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:background} reviews federated optimization, personalized federated learning, and generative approaches to client heterogeneity. Section~\ref{sec:method} introduces the FusedSpaceFed architecture and training protocol. Section~\ref{sec:theory} presents a fixed-round analysis of classifier-parameter gradient dispersion under dual-space fusion. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the experimental evaluation on medical datasets and FEMNIST. Finally, Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the results, limitations, and future directions.

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:background}

Federated learning is affected by both statistical heterogeneity and partial client participation. FedAvg performs repeated local optimization followed by server-side parameter averaging~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}. FedProx adds a proximal penalty that limits the distance between local and global parameters~\cite{li2018federated}, while SCAFFOLD uses client and server control variates to correct local update drift~\cite{karimireddy2020scaffold}. FedDyn introduces a dynamic regularizer, MOON aligns local and global representations through contrastive learning, and FedNTD preserves global knowledge by distilling predictions associated with non-ground-truth classes~\cite{acar2021feddyn,li2021moon,lee2022preservation}.

A closely related research direction is personalized federated learning, in which only part of the model is globally shared. FedPer keeps personalization layers local while federating a common base network~\cite{arivazhagan2019fedper}. FedRep instead learns a shared representation together with client-specific prediction heads~\cite{collins2021fedrep}. Ditto jointly learns a global model and a regularized personalized model for each client~\cite{li2021ditto}. FedBN retains client-specific batch-normalization components to address feature-distribution shifts, including the acquisition shifts that may arise across medical imaging sites~\cite{li2021fedbn}. These works establish that separating private and shared components is an existing federated learning paradigm. FusedSpaceFed differs in the direction of this separation: the multiscale encoder is private, the decoder and classifier are shared, and the reconstructed signal is added to the original input before classification.

Representation alignment has also been addressed by methods such as MOON and FedNTD, which introduce auxiliary objectives at the feature or prediction level. By contrast, FusedSpaceFed does not add a representation-alignment penalty to the classification objective. It uses an architectural pathway and a reconstruction warm-up to construct a client-conditioned fused input.
%More broadly, GloNet~\cite{dicecco2024glonets} illustrates how architectural connectivity can alter the effective representation exposed to a classifier, although it addresses centralized rather than federated training.
More broadly, prior centralized work has explored structural interventions at several levels of a neural network. GloNets modifies global connectivity so that the network head can receive information from different depths~\cite{dicecco2024glonets}. A related line of work on dendrite-activated and multi-bias units modifies the basic computational unit and its preactivation pattern to preserve information flow and improve parameter efficiency~\cite{metta2024increasingbiases,metta2025dac,metta2025increasingbiases}. SwitchPath instead changes the activation mechanism, stochastically exposing alternative information pathways during training; the original method and its subsequent theoretical and empirical analysis are reported in~\cite{dicecco2025switchpath,dicecco2025switchpathjournal}. Although these approaches were developed for centralized learning, they illustrate the broader principle that the computational pathway itself can be modified to shape representation learning, rather than acting only through the training objective.

Generative federated methods use learned generators or autoencoders to exchange information without directly sharing raw samples. FedCVAE uses conditional variational autoencoders to generate representative data~\cite{tu2021fedcvae}; SDA-FL constructs synthetic datasets that are pseudo-labelled and redistributed~\cite{qu2022sdafl}; and FissionVAE partitions generative components across client clusters~\cite{zhang2024fissionvae}. These methods primarily use generative models for sample synthesis. FusedSpaceFed instead retains the reconstruction pathway at both training and inference time and uses its output directly in the classifier input. FusedSpaceFed thereby defines a personalized dual-space architecture
in which the reconstruction pathway remains active during both training and
inference.

\subsection{Federated objective and gradient dispersion}

Let $\theta$ denote the parameters of a shared classifier and let
$\mathcal{S}_t$ be the set of clients participating at communication round~$t$,
with $m_t=|\mathcal{S}_t|$. For a scalar task loss $\ell_{\mathrm{task}}$, the
local objective of client~$i$ is
\begin{equation*}
F_i(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}_i}
\left[
\ell_{\mathrm{task}}\!\left(\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(x),y\right)
\right].
\end{equation*}
All client gradients must be evaluated at a common parameter vector in order
for their dispersion to be meaningful. At a reference point $\theta_t$, define
\begin{align*}
g_{i,t} &:= \nabla_{\theta}F_i(\theta)\big|_{\theta=\theta_t},\\
\bar{g}_t &:= \frac{1}{m_t}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}g_{i,t}.
\end{align*}
The corresponding gradient dissimilarity is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gradient_dissimilarity}
\Gamma_t
=
\frac{1}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}
\left\|g_{i,t}-\bar{g}_t\right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
The quantity $\Gamma_t$ measures the dispersion of client gradients evaluated
at the common classifier state $\theta_t$. Lower values correspond to more
closely aligned client update directions, whereas larger values indicate
stronger disagreement among the local objectives.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

We now describe the FusedSpaceFed architecture, using the example of medical images as a setting, even though the architecture is not setting-specific. Our design combines a convolutional autoencoder with a classifier trained on fused representations $x + \tilde{x}$. The decoder and classifier are shared and synchronized across clients, while the encoder remains local to preserve data-specific adaptation. This section formalizes the model structure, loss objectives and aggregation mechanism. We also detail implementation features such as encoder warm-up phases and architecture-specific choices aligned with imaging benchmarks. An overview of the full training algorithm is provided in Algorithm~\ref{alg:fusedspacefed}.

\subsection{Dual-Space Architecture}

Consider $K$ clients, where client~$i$ has access to
$\mathcal{D}_i=\{(x_j^{(i)},y_j^{(i)})\}_{j=1}^{n_i}$ and
$x_j^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{c_{\mathrm{in}}\times H\times W}$. The number of
input channels $c_{\mathrm{in}}$ is dataset-dependent.

\paragraph{Private multiscale encoder.}
Client~$i$ maintains a private convolutional encoder $E_i$. Because the
implementation uses a compact U-Net with skip connections~\cite{ronneberger2015unet}, the encoder output
is a tuple of multiscale feature maps rather than a single latent vector:
\begin{equation*}
E_i(x)
=
h_i(x)
=
\left(s_i^{(1)}(x),s_i^{(2)}(x),z_i(x)\right),
\end{equation*}
where $s_i^{(1)}$ and $s_i^{(2)}$ are the two skip representations and
\begin{equation*}
z_i(x)\in
\mathbb{R}^{d_z\times (H/4)\times(W/4)}
\end{equation*}
is the bottleneck tensor. Accordingly, $d_z$ denotes the number of bottleneck
channels, not the total scalar dimension of the latent representation.

\paragraph{Shared decoder.}
The decoder $D$ receives the complete multiscale representation:
\begin{equation*}
\tilde{x}_i
=
D\!\left(h_i(x)\right)
=
D\!\left(s_i^{(1)}(x),s_i^{(2)}(x),z_i(x)\right),
\end{equation*}
with $\tilde{x}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{c_{\mathrm{in}}\times H\times W}$. Local
copies of the decoder are initialized from the shared decoder at the beginning
of each communication round.

\paragraph{Shared classifier and fusion.}
The classifier $\mathcal{C}$ is a ResNet20-v2~\cite{he2016identity} that maps the fused image to
$c_{\mathrm{out}}$ logits:
\begin{equation*}
x_{\mathrm{fused}}^{(i)}
=
x+\tilde{x}_i, \,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,
o_i
=
\mathcal{C}\!\left(x_{\mathrm{fused}}^{(i)}\right)
\in\mathbb{R}^{c_{\mathrm{out}}}.
\end{equation*}
For multiclass datasets, $c_{\mathrm{out}}$ is the number of mutually
exclusive classes. For ChestMNIST, it is the number of independently predicted
labels. Additive fusion preserves the spatial dimensions and number of channels
of the original input.

\subsection{Training Objectives}\label{sec:training_obj}

Local optimization consists of two consecutive phases in every communication
round. The two losses are not combined into a weighted joint objective.

\paragraph{Phase 1: encoder reconstruction warm-up.}
At the beginning of round~$t$, client~$i$ receives the shared decoder
$D_{\phi_t}$ and classifier $\mathcal{C}_{\theta_t}$. Both components are
frozen, and only the private encoder parameters $\psi_i$ are updated for
$E_w$ epochs using
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}^{(i)}
=
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_i}
\left[
\left\|
x-D_{\phi_t}\!\left(E_{\psi_i}(x)\right)
\right\|_2^2
\right].
\end{equation*}
This phase adapts the private encoder to the decoder received at the current
round, establishing a reconstruction-consistent initialization for the
next classification phase.

\paragraph{Phase 2: end-to-end classification.}
After warm-up, client~$i$ unfreezes its local decoder and classifier and updates
the encoder, decoder, and classifier for $E$ local epochs using only
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}^{(i)}
=
\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}_i}
\left[
\ell_{\mathrm{task}}
\left(
\mathcal{C}_{\theta_i}
\left(
x+D_{\phi_i}\!\left(E_{\psi_i}(x)\right)
\right),
y
\right)
\right].
\end{equation*}
No pixel-level reconstruction term is included in Phase~2. Gradients of
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}^{(i)}$ propagate through the fused input and update
the classifier, decoder, and private encoder.
For the multiclass MedMNIST tasks and FEMNIST,
$\ell_{\mathrm{task}}$ is the softmax cross-entropy loss. ChestMNIST is a
multi-label task and uses binary cross-entropy with logits for each label.
The reconstruction loss in Phase~1 is mean squared error for every dataset.

\subsection{Global Aggregation}\label{sec:aggregation}

Let $\mathcal{S}_t$ denote the active clients at round~$t$ and
$m_t=|\mathcal{S}_t|$. After local training, every active client sends its
updated decoder parameters $\phi_i^{t+1}$ and classifier parameters
$\theta_i^{t+1}$ to the server. FusedSpaceFed uses uniform client averaging:
\begin{align}
\phi_{t+1}
&=
\frac{1}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}\phi_i^{t+1},
\label{eq:agg_dec}\\
\theta_{t+1}
&=
\frac{1}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}\theta_i^{t+1}.
\label{eq:agg_cls}
\end{align}
The choice of uniform averaging is intentional and is also used for the
federated baselines. It therefore assigns equal aggregation weight to every
participating client rather than weighting updates by local sample count.
Private encoder parameters $\psi_i$ are retained by their corresponding
clients and are never transmitted or averaged. When a client participates
again, it resumes from its previously stored private encoder and combines it
with the latest shared decoder and classifier.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{FusedSpaceFed training protocol}
\label{alg:fusedspacefed}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Initialize:} shared decoder $\phi_0$, shared classifier
$\theta_0$, and one persistent private encoder $\psi_i^0$ for each client
\For{communication round $t=0,\ldots,T-1$}
    \State Select the active client set $\mathcal{S}_t$
    \State Broadcast $(\phi_t,\theta_t)$ to all $i\in\mathcal{S}_t$
    \For{each $i\in\mathcal{S}_t$ in parallel}
        \State Initialize local decoder $\phi_i\gets\phi_t$ and classifier
        $\theta_i\gets\theta_t$
        \State Restore the persistent private encoder $\psi_i$
        \State Freeze $(\phi_i,\theta_i)$
        \State Update only $\psi_i$ for $E_w$ epochs using
        $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}^{(i)}$
        \State Unfreeze $(\phi_i,\theta_i)$
        \State Update $(\psi_i,\phi_i,\theta_i)$ for $E$ epochs using only
        $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}^{(i)}$
        \State Store $\psi_i$ locally
        \State Send $(\phi_i,\theta_i)$ to the server
    \EndFor
    \State $\displaystyle
    \phi_{t+1}\gets\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_t|}
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}\phi_i$
    \State $\displaystyle
    \theta_{t+1}\gets\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_t|}
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}\theta_i$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Personalized Inference}\label{sec:inference}

FusedSpaceFed defines a family of client-conditioned predictors composed of a
shared decoder and classifier together with one private encoder for each
participating client. For client~$i$, prediction uses
\begin{equation*}
\hat{y}_i
=
\mathcal{C}_{\theta_T}
\left(
x+D_{\phi_T}\!\left(E_{\psi_i^T}(x)\right)
\right).
\end{equation*}
The resulting predictor is client-conditioned: the decoder and classifier are
shared, whereas the encoder is associated with the client that owns it.
For a previously unseen client, a new private encoder can be initialized and
adapted through reconstruction warm-up using the final shared decoder. The
evaluation presented here focuses on the client-conditioned predictors learned
for participating clients.

\section{Gradient-Dispersion Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

We analyze how dual-space fusion changes the dispersion of
classifier-parameter gradients across clients. At communication round~$t$, the
analysis conditions on the current private encoders, shared decoder, and shared
classifier, allowing the original-input and fused-input gradient fields to be
compared at the same model state.

\subsection{Original and Fused Client Objectives}

Fix a communication round~$t$, an active client set $\mathcal{S}_t$, the shared
classifier parameters $\theta_t$, the shared decoder parameters $\phi_t$, and
the current private encoder parameters $\psi_{i,t}$. During this analysis, all
of these quantities are held fixed except for the argument $\theta$ with
respect to which classifier gradients are defined.
For client~$i$, define the reference original-input objective
\begin{equation*}
F_{i,t}^{\mathrm{orig}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}_i}
\left[
\ell_{\mathrm{task}}
\left(
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(x),y
\right)
\right]
\end{equation*}
and the fused-input objective
\begin{equation*}
F_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fused}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}_i}
\left[
\ell_{\mathrm{task}}
\left(
\mathcal{C}_{\theta}
\left(
x+D_{\phi_t}\!\left(E_{\psi_{i,t}}(x)\right)
\right),
y
\right)
\right].
\end{equation*}
Both are scalar loss functions, and their gradients are taken with respect to
the same classifier parameter vector:
\begin{equation*}
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{orig}}
=
\nabla_{\theta}F_{i,t}^{\mathrm{orig}}(\theta)
\big|_{\theta=\theta_t}, \,\,\,\,\,\,\,
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fused}}
=
\nabla_{\theta}F_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fused}}(\theta)
\big|_{\theta=\theta_t}.
\end{equation*}
Define the fusion-induced gradient difference
\begin{equation*}
b_{i,t}
=
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fused}}
-
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{orig}}.
\end{equation*}
Let
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{g}_t^{s}
&= \frac{1}{m_t}
   \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t} g_{i,t}^{s},
   \quad s\in\{\mathrm{orig},\mathrm{fused}\},\\
\bar{b}_t
&= \frac{1}{m_t}
   \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t} b_{i,t}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where $m_t=|\mathcal{S}_t|$.

\subsection{Exact Fixed-Round Decomposition}

The original and fused classifier-gradient dispersions are
\begin{align*}
\Gamma_t^{\mathrm{orig}}
&=
\frac{1}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}
\left\|
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{orig}}
-
\bar{g}_t^{\mathrm{orig}}
\right\|_2^2,\\
\Gamma_t^{\mathrm{fused}}
&=
\frac{1}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}
\left\|
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fused}}
-
\bar{g}_t^{\mathrm{fused}}
\right\|_2^2.
\end{align*}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-round gradient-dispersion identity]
\label{prop:fixed_round_decomposition}
At any fixed round~$t$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact_decomp}
\Gamma_t^{\mathrm{fused}}
=
\Gamma_t^{\mathrm{orig}}
+
B_t
+
\Phi_t.
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
B_t
&=
\frac{1}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}
\left\|
b_{i,t}-\bar{b}_t
\right\|_2^2
\geq 0,\\
\Phi_t
&=
\frac{2}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}
\left\langle
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{orig}}-\bar{g}_t^{\mathrm{orig}},
b_{i,t}-\bar{b}_t
\right\rangle .
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Because
\begin{equation*}
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{fused}}
-
\bar{g}_t^{\mathrm{fused}}
=
\left(
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{orig}}
-
\bar{g}_t^{\mathrm{orig}}
\right)
+
\left(
b_{i,t}-\bar{b}_t
\right),
\end{equation*}
expanding the squared Euclidean norm and averaging over
$i\in\mathcal{S}_t$ gives
\begin{align*}
\Gamma_t^{\mathrm{fused}}
&=
\frac{1}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}
\left\|
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{orig}}
-
\bar{g}_t^{\mathrm{orig}}
\right\|_2^2\\
&\quad+
\frac{1}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}
\left\|
b_{i,t}-\bar{b}_t
\right\|_2^2\\
&\quad+
\frac{2}{m_t}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t}
\left\langle
g_{i,t}^{\mathrm{orig}}-\bar{g}_t^{\mathrm{orig}},
b_{i,t}-\bar{b}_t
\right\rangle\\
&=
\Gamma_t^{\mathrm{orig}}+B_t+\Phi_t.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-round reduction condition]
\label{cor:fixed_round_reduction}
At the fixed state considered above,
\begin{equation*}
\Gamma_t^{\mathrm{fused}}
<
\Gamma_t^{\mathrm{orig}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
B_t+\Phi_t<0.
\end{equation*}
\end{corollary}

The term $B_t$ measures the additional cross-client variability introduced by
the fusion-induced gradient differences. The cross-term $\Phi_t$ is negative
when these differences tend to oppose the original client deviations from
their mean. Therefore, fusion is associated with lower dispersion only when
the negative cross-covariance is sufficiently large to compensate for
$B_t$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:exact_decomp} shows that the effect of fusion is determined
by the interaction between the variability of the fusion-induced gradient
difference, represented by $B_t$, and its cross-client covariance with the
original gradient deviations, represented by $\Phi_t$. The experimental
analysis in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} evaluates the resulting
classifier-gradient dispersion $\Gamma_t$ at the final trained state of each
method.

\subsection{Communication Complexity}

Let $P_C$ and $P_D$ denote the numbers of transmitted scalar values in the
classifier and decoder states, respectively, and let $m_t=|\mathcal{S}_t|$.
At round~$t$, the server sends one classifier and one decoder state to every
active client, and every active client returns the corresponding updated
states. The resulting bidirectional communication is
\begin{equation*}
\mathrm{Comm}_t^{\mathrm{FusedSpaceFed}}
=
2m_t(P_C+P_D)
\end{equation*}
transmitted scalar values, or
$
2m_t(P_C+P_D)b
$
bytes, when each scalar occupies $b$ bytes.

The private encoder is neither uploaded nor downloaded. Relative to FedAvg and
FedProx using the same classifier, FusedSpaceFed therefore communicates an
additional $2m_tP_D$ scalar values per round. For the convolutional decoder,
$P_D$ is determined by the channel widths, convolutional kernels, upsampling
layers, and skip-connection processing blocks. SCAFFOLD additionally maintains
client and server control variates, with communication determined by the
specific control-variate implementation.


\section{Experimental Evaluation}\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate FusedSpaceFed on a diverse set of medical imaging tasks~\cite{medmnistv2}, a standardized suite of biomedical datasets spanning multiple modalities (X-ray, OCT, CT, fundus, dermatoscopy, microscopy), and FEMNIST~\cite{caldas2018leaf}, a popular benchmark for a federated learning scenario.

For the main MedMNIST experiments, we distribute the official training split
across 10 clients using a class-wise Dirichlet allocation with
$\alpha\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,1.0\}$. Lower values of $\alpha$ produce more
concentrated client label distributions. Every official training example is
assigned to exactly one client.
We compare FusedSpaceFed with three established federated learning reference methods:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FedAvg}~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}: Standard baseline using parameter averaging.
\item \textbf{FedProx}~\cite{li2018federated}: Adds a proximal term to stabilize local updates.
\item \textbf{SCAFFOLD}~\cite{karimireddy2020scaffold}: Uses control variates to correct client drift.
\end{itemize}

All methods use the same ResNet20-v2 classifier architecture. Within each
independent run, each method starts from the
same classifier initialization and uses the same client partition. FusedSpaceFed
additionally uses the compact U-Net architecture described in
Section~\ref{sec:method}.

MedMNIST images are loaded from the official $64\times64$ release and resized
to $32\times32$. Training uses 50 communication rounds, 3 local classification
epochs per round, and full participation of all medical clients.
FusedSpaceFed performs one additional encoder warm-up epoch at the beginning of
each round. The MedMNIST batch size is 128. The classifier is optimized using
SGD with learning rate $0.01$, while the encoder and decoder are optimized
using Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam} with learning rate $0.001$. FedProx uses proximal coefficient
$\mu=0.01$. Client states are aggregated using a uniform client average.

Results are averaged over five independent runs with different seeds. The official test split is evaluated only after the
final communication round. The full implementation, data-partitioning procedure, and experiment configurations are available in the supplementary material.
For MedMNIST, FedAvg, FedProx and SCAFFOLD are evaluated using their final
shared classifier. For FusedSpaceFed, each of the 10 private client encoders is
combined with the final shared decoder and classifier and evaluated on the
official test split. The reported FusedSpaceFed result is the uniform mean of
the metrics obtained by these 10 client-conditioned pipelines.

The bottleneck width was selected from
$d_z\in\{8,16,32,64\}$ using the official validation split. Selection was
performed separately for each MedMNIST dataset using the validation accuracy
of FusedSpaceFed. The selected value was then fixed for all test
evaluations and all heterogeneity settings of that dataset. The resulting values are reported in the
``Best $d_z$'' columns of Tables~\ref{tab:performance} and
\ref{tab:performance_1}.

ChestMNIST is multi-label. To construct its Dirichlet client partition, each
training example is assigned a single proxy stratum: the first positive label
is used when at least one finding is present, and samples without any positive
finding are assigned to an additional ``no finding'' stratum. This proxy is
used only for partitioning. The original multi-label target vector is retained
for training and evaluation, which use binary cross-entropy with logits.
For ChestMNIST, predictions are obtained by thresholding each sigmoid output at
$0.5$, and accuracy denotes the mean element-wise agreement across samples and
labels.

In addition to the five Dirichlet settings used in the main experiments, we

\alpha_0 &= 0.05,\\
\alpha_{j+1} &= \alpha_j + 0.10,

This twelve-point grid corresponds to the values displayed in

This produces increasingly heterogeneous client partitions, where lower $\alpha$ values yield stronger class imbalance and more degenerate client distributions.

We train the four methods on PathMNIST for every

\subsection{FEMNIST Benchmark}

We further evaluate all methods on \textbf{FEMNIST} from the \textsc{LEAF} benchmark~\cite{caldas2018leaf}, a widely adopted reference dataset for federated learning. FEMNIST consists of handwritten characters from thousands of distinct writers and exhibits natural client-level heterogeneity, as each client corresponds to a single user with highly skewed label and style distributions. This setting closely reflects realistic federated scenarios and is commonly used to assess robustness to client drift and non-IID data.

FEMNIST uses the natural writer-level partition supplied by LEAF: each writer
is treated as one client, and writer data are not pooled or repartitioned. At
each communication round, a subset of writers is sampled and performs local
training. Private FusedSpaceFed encoders and SCAFFOLD client control variates
persist between successive participations of the same writer.

FedAvg, FedProx and SCAFFOLD use a shared classifier, whereas FusedSpaceFed
combines the shared decoder and classifier with writer-specific private
encoders. We therefore report writer-conditioned personalized test
performance: the test examples of each writer are processed using that
writer's private encoder together with the final shared decoder and classifier.

Gradient dissimilarity is evaluated after the final round using one
deterministic batch from each of 50 seeded writers and gradients with respect
to the classifier parameters only. Table~\ref{tab:femnist_results} reports the
mean results over five independent runs. FusedSpaceFed obtains the highest
writer-conditioned accuracy and the lowest measured final-round classifier
gradient dissimilarity among the evaluated methods.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}


We presented \emph{FusedSpaceFed}, a personalized federated learning framework
based on dual-space input fusion. Each client retains a private multiscale
encoder, while the decoder and classifier are synchronized through federated
aggregation. Local training alternates an encoder reconstruction warm-up
with an end-to-end classification phase in which gradients update the encoder,
decoder, and classifier through the fused input.

We characterized the method using an exact fixed-round decomposition of
classifier-parameter gradient dispersion. The decomposition separates the
dispersion obtained from the original inputs from a non-negative
fusion-induced variance term and a cross-covariance term, identifying the
condition under which the fused pathway improves cross-client gradient
alignment at a given model state.

Across the evaluated settings, FusedSpaceFed achieves
higher average predictive performance than the considered reference methods in
most of the reported non-IID configurations. The final-round gradient
measurements also show lower classifier-gradient dispersion for FusedSpaceFed
on the evaluated subset of datasets.

\subsection{Current Limitations}

FusedSpaceFed requires a persistent private encoder for every participating
client. Consequently, the shared decoder and classifier do not constitute a
complete standalone inference model. Evaluation on a previously unseen client
would require adapting a new private encoder, a setting not investigated in
this work.

The dual-space architecture also increases local computation and memory usage
because clients train both an autoencoder pathway and a classifier. It
additionally communicates the decoder state at every round. The main
architecture-dependent hyperparameter is the bottleneck width $d_z$, which is
selected using validation data.

The method has been evaluated on image-based tasks using convolutional
architectures. Its applicability to other modalities and model families
remains to be established. The present analysis focuses on gradient dispersion
at individual communication rounds; extending it to the joint evolution of
the private encoders, shared decoder and shared classifier is an important
direction for future work.

\subsection{Future Research Directions}

Several directions could improve both applicability and theoretical understanding. A primary objective is to remove the dependency on client-specific encoders through mechanisms such as encoder distillation, progressive encoder alignment, or encoder-free inference strategies that allow a single deployable global model. Reducing computational costs through adaptive or resource-aware architectures is also important for scaling to large federations and, more broadly, for sustainable AI systems~\cite{amato2024sustainability}. Self-scaling architectures provide an example of this direction: SCALE-RL dynamically adapts network depth during training to reduce computation and energy requirements~\cite{veglio2026scalerl}.

Extending the framework to heterogeneous or multi-modal data and developing
analyses that account for finite samples and the joint evolution of all model
components would strengthen the foundations of the approach. Support for hierarchical or clustered federations and validation in production-like environments with realistic communication and hardware constraints are further practical steps.

Another direction is to study the interaction between representation-level mechanisms and adaptive optimization dynamics. Recent optimizers that switch update rules according to local gradient and curvature information provide one example of how optimization dynamics can be modified to address unfavorable training regimes~\cite{dicecco2027eva}.

The dual-space formulation opens a broader research perspective: explicitly learning complementary representation spaces to align local and global objectives may provide benefits beyond the setting studied here. Exploring this principle in other distributed learning scenarios represents a promising direction for future work.
